04 · How it got here

Two lines: the physics sims, then the content pipeline

Each stop is a dated step. Filled stops are where the plan changed. Commit links go to the public repo, so those steps can be checked.

Line 1 · The physics sims, May to September 2026

  1. By 9 May

    Sims that teach from the code up

    All 76 original sims got a plain-language comment pass, so the code explains the physics. The same day, a new batch aimed at known exam traps was added.

  2. 12 May

    Checked against the textbook

    44 sims cross-checked against the course textbook: zero formula errors.

  3. 23 May

    A full correctness audit

    All 73 sims of the time audited; no new bugs found.

  4. 30–31 May

    Audit by exam topic

    The 33 sims for the later tests were re-audited topic by topic: 29 bugs fixed, and an automated checker caught 2 more.

  5. 1–5 Jun

    Checked against the course

    14 sims re-checked against what the first test covers: 17 fixes.

  6. 13 Jun

    One look

    Every sim and study page moved to one shared dark palette.

  7. 9–13 Jul

    Live checks before each test

    A parallel audit, then a pass in the browser on the running sims: 12 more fixes.

  8. 18 Jul

    The first animated explainers

    Three story-style explainers (lenses, mirrors, relativity) that play scene by scene with captions.

  9. 4 Sep

    97 pages, one hub

    94 sims and 3 explainers; 89 are linked from the hub page. 111 fixes are logged so none of them comes back.

    Change here: the three explainers start line 2.

Line 2 · The content pipeline, 5 to 7 October 2026

  1. 5 Oct

    The brief, and an honest inventory

    The ask: engaging content made with AI tools, on a weekly goal. The explainers and course pages existed, but none were online; only a separate demo sim was public.

    Decision: put a real sample online before replying, instead of describing it.

  2. 6 Oct · 23:21

    Three explainers go live

    Fixed: the work became linkable. commit c77d11f

  3. 6 Oct · late

    Measure before rebuilding

    The lens explainer's first scene took 29.7 ms per frame against a 16.7 ms budget, so it stuttered. The cause was a glow effect redrawn on every frame, not the drawing engine.

    Decision: keep the engine, fix the glow, and rebuild the story through a one-folder pipeline with a check per stage. The folder shape is borrowed from Jake Van Clief's one-folder animation workflow, without its voice and render stages for now.

  4. 7 Oct · 01:06

    Lens Story v2 ships after three review rounds

    One round caught the first scene reading as an expanding box instead of light: every ray grew by the same fraction, so they all finished together. The shared rule now says rays grow by distance. A second model reviewed the result and passed it; the heaviest scene is now 1.6 ms.

    Fixed: the stutter, and a rule every later story inherits. commit 8e4165f

  5. 7 Oct · 20:00

    The process gets a diagram

    Fixed: the method was visible only as its product. commit c76af63

  6. 7 Oct · 21:11

    The same checks, aimed at image and video

    Fixed: answers "can this do image and video?" with a plan that is labelled proposed, not claimed. commit 5805861

  7. 7 Oct · evening

    The first video was dropped

    It was a 30-second screen recording of the lens explainer. A recording of a web page is not made for the frame, and it read as low quality.

    Decision: make a video composed for a vertical screen, voice first, with captions timed to the spoken words. The plan is on page 6.

  8. 7 Oct · night

    This site You are here

    The diagrams were rewritten as plain text in larger type with calmer colours, so they read on a phone.

    Decision: let the workflow itself be the sample, with every step traceable.
