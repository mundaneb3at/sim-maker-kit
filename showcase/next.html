A 60-second "how this was made" video

Vertical, 1080 × 1920, about 150 spoken words. The one message: I don't make the content by hand; I run the pipeline that makes it, and I read what each stage made before the next one runs.

  1. Voice first

    The script is narrated by a local AI voice before any picture is made.

  2. Words, timed

    Every word gets a timestamp from the audio, and every cut and caption is anchored to a word.

  3. Cues

    One cue per picture change, each naming a real asset: the folder tree, a stage contract, the lens explainer, a diagram.

  4. One scene, four looks

    The same scene shown in 16-bit, clean, blueprint and hand-drawn styles. The storyboard never names a colour, so the look is a late, cheap choice.

  5. Render and check

    58 to 62 seconds, audio in sync at three spot checks, then watched start to end before it goes anywhere.

Generated clips and a 3D look

Two or three short clips from a video-generation model on its free tier open the video and show the pipeline pointed at video. They will be labelled as a first run, because they are one.

Alongside that, a study of how short AI-made series get their 3D look: shot by shot, what makes it read as 3D and what most likely made it, then the options for this pipeline with cost and risk. It is research, not a verdict.

If the generated clips don't work out, the video still ships on the voice, captions and real project footage.
